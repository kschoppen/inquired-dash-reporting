Status Quo
🧩
Districts cobbling programs together / Workshop-style curriculum
The largest "competitor" by volume. Many K–5 ELA decisions default to patchwork — existing basal + supplemental phonics kit + teacher-created read-alouds. Workshop-model districts (Lucy Calkins legacy) are actively replacing but often land on a known brand rather than something new. This is the inertia Inkwell has to overcome, not just a named competitor. Key wedge: science of reading mandates and state legislative pressure on districts to move off unaligned programs.

Strategic Opportunities · By Product
Inkwell
Get on the state lists before 2027–28 procurement closes around them
Pennsylvania's Act 135 list already puts five competitors in front of every PA K–3 buyer ahead of the 2027–28 mandate, and California's SBE vote (Nov 12–13) will do the same there. At the same time the market is converging on one vendor per literacy block: Great Minds says districts are "requesting one product to guide our literacy block," Dragonfly pitches K–12 with StudySync, and CKLA now runs Skills through grade 5. Inkwell's integrated ELA + social studies knowledge-building is the story none of them can tell in K–2, but it needs list status and a full-block answer to get evaluated at all.
